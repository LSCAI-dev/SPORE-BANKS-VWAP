Trade setup — A & B

Trading rules — Setups A & B, order flow

Entry (long) — all 3 on a daily close:

  1. Location (AVWAP): close above anchored VWAP, or reclaims it. In an uptrend, a pullback that tags AVWAP or −1σ and closes back above also qualifies.
  2. Momentum (MACD 12,26,9): histogram > 0 and rising. Stronger if the MACD line is also above 0.
  3. Timing (Stoch 14,3,3): %K above %D after dipping below 50 (best from below 20). No new entries with %K > 80.

Execution: buy on a break above the signal bar's high. Stop: below the nearest support under entry (−1σ band or 15-day swing low) less a 0.3% buffer; if the anchor is under 10 bars old, use the swing low and make the 15-day high the second target. Size: (capital × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop).

Selling:

  • +1σ hit → take ⅓–½ off, move stop to entry.
  • +2σ hit → take the rest, or trail the stop under swing lows.
  • %K crosses below %D above 80 → tighten stop.
  • MACD histogram turns negative → exit remainder.
  • Close back below AVWAP after entry → exit (thesis failed).
  • No progress in 10 bars → exit.

Setup B (mean reversion): only when price is at or below −1σ. Entry needs the MACD histogram to turn positive with %K > %D from below 20. Stop below the 15-day swing low. Targets −1σ, then AVWAP.

Status: ENTRY = all 3 filters pass · WAIT = some pass · STAND ASIDE = below AVWAP and MACD histogram negative.

Order flow vs AVWAP — how to read it

The two questions. AVWAP answers “where is price compared with the average price paid since the dividend announcement?” Order flow answers “who is pushing — buyers or sellers?” Read together, they tell you whether a move is real or being quietly resisted.

Bid vs ask volume, in plain terms.

  • Ask % = transactions at the seller's price (the ask) ÷ total volume. The buyer was impatient and paid what the seller asked, using up the offers and pushing price up. Counts as buying pressure.
  • Bid % = transactions at the buyer's price (the bid) ÷ total volume. The seller was impatient and accepted what the buyer bid, using up the bids and pushing price down. Counts as selling pressure.
  • Ask % + Bid % = 100%.
  • The patient side (orders waiting in the queue) is “passive”. A large passive order can soak up the impatient side without price moving — this is called absorption. Example: Ask % stays high but price does not rise, because a big seller keeps refilling the ask — the passive seller is controlling price.

The numbers in the box.

  • Order flow chart = the blue line is Ask % over the last 5 sessions (volume-weighted). Each single day's split is shown in the price chart's volume bars (teal = at the ask, red = at the bid). The axis reads Ask % / Bid %, e.g. 60/40.
    • Ask ≥ 60% (teal zone) = buying pressure — buyers paying the seller's price.
    • Bid ≥ 60% (red zone, i.e. Ask ≤ 40%) = selling pressure — sellers accepting the buyer's price.
    • In between = balanced.
    • High Ask % while price is flat or falling = absorption — a passive seller controlling price (shown in the Order flow box). High Bid % while price holds = a passive buyer absorbing.
  • Delta = ask volume − bid volume for the day (shares). Positive = more traded at the seller's price.
  • Anchored CVD = all daily deltas added up from the AVWAP anchor date. It is the running score of buyers vs sellers for the same group of holders the AVWAP measures. The % shows how lopsided it is relative to all volume traded since the anchor.
  • Ask vol % = share of volume traded at the seller's price. Ask ≥ 60% buying pressure · between balanced · Bid ≥ 60% (Ask ≤ 40%) selling pressure.

Simple analogy: price is a car, AVWAP is the road's centre line, order flow is the accelerator. Car ahead of the line with foot on the gas = healthy. Car ahead but foot on the brake and still not slowing = something is pushing it from behind (absorption). Car behind the line, foot on the gas but not moving = something is holding it back.

Extra flags in the box.

  • AVWAP test — held: the day touched AVWAP, closed above it, and delta was positive. Buyers defended the average price. Good for Setup A.
  • AVWAP test — broke: the day touched AVWAP, closed below it, and delta was negative. Sellers won the fight; treat AVWAP as resistance until reclaimed.
  • Bearish divergence: price makes a higher 10-day high but CVD makes a lower high — the rise has fewer buyers behind it. Tighten stops, especially above +1σ.
  • Bullish divergence: price makes a lower 10-day low but CVD makes a higher low — selling is drying up. Watch for a Setup B trigger.

How it fits the 3 filters. Order flow is a confirmation, not a 4th entry filter. Treat it as: ENTRY + matching order flow (row 1) = higher confidence; ENTRY + opposing order flow (row 2 or 3) = smaller size or wait one more bar; any divergence = tighten stops.

Data quality. Solid bars use real time & sales from your broker (uploaded to tns/). Faded bars are estimates from where the close sits in the day's range — a close near the high is assumed mostly buying. Estimates are rough and can be wrong on reversal days; trust solid bars more. Auction trades (before 09:00 and from 17:00) are excluded because they have no buyer/seller side.

Rules-based screen only, not advice. Verify levels on your broker terminal before acting.

Trade setup — C

Trading rules — Setup C

Setup C — Claude for SGX banks (trend + participation). Long when all 5 pass on a daily close:

  1. ADX (14) > 20 and +DI > −DI — a strong uptrend. ADX (gold line, dashed threshold at 20) measures trend strength in either direction; +DI (teal) above −DI (red) confirms the direction is up. ADX > 20 with −DI above +DI is a strong downtrend and does not qualify.
  2. EMA 8 > EMA 21 — short-term trend up (white and purple lines on the price chart). Crossovers are marked with a bold ✚ at the crossing point: teal ✚ = EMA 8 crosses above EMA 21 (bullish), red ✚ = EMA 8 crosses below (bearish).
  3. 5-day average volume > 20-day average volume — participation is rising (blue 5-day and orange 20-day lines on the volume bars).
  4. Up/down volume ratio > 1.3× — over the last 20 sessions, volume on up-closes is at least 1.3 × volume on down-closes (up-close = close above the previous close).
  5. OBV at its 20-day high — On-Balance Volume (adds the day's volume on an up-close, subtracts it on a down-close) is at or above its highest value of the previous 20 sessions, today excluded (dashed green line, OBV pane) — i.e. OBV is making a new 20-session high.

Setup C exits (checked on each daily close after entry):

  1. Close < EMA 8 → sell half the holding.
  2. Close < EMA 21 → sell all that remains.
  3. Stop: initial 1.5 × ATR, then 2 × ATR trail → sell all if the day's low touches the stop. At entry the stop is the entry close − 1.5 × ATR (14). It then trails at the highest high since entry − 2 × ATR (14), taking over once that is higher than the initial stop. The stop only moves up, never down. Shown as the orange dotted line.
  4. MACD histogram falls 3 bars in a row → tighten the trail to 1 × ATR (highest high − 1 × ATR) for the rest of the trade.

Whichever full exit comes first (EMA 21 or the stop) closes the trade. The app tracks a hypothetical position from each Setup C LONG signal (bought at that day's close) so the current trail and exit status are always shown; markers on the price chart: ▲ C buy, ½ half sold, T trail tightened, ▼ Exit.

The bank card shows how many of the 5 pass (e.g. 3 / 5) and turns to LONG when all 5 pass. Setup C is independent of the AVWAP filters used by Setups A and B.
